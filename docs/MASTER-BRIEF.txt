# 3. CORE PRODUCT PRINCIPLES

Every implementation decision should follow these principles.

## 3.1 Learning quality over gamification

Gamification should support learning, not replace it.

Points, streaks, XP, levels, achievements, progress bars, and similar elements are useful, but actual language competence is the main objective.

## 3.2 Active recall

The learner should frequently have to produce an answer rather than simply recognize one.

## 3.3 Spaced repetition

Vocabulary, grammar mistakes, phrases, and difficult concepts should return automatically based on performance.

## 3.4 Adaptive difficulty

The system should become aware of:

- strengths
- weaknesses
- repeated mistakes
- forgotten vocabulary
- grammar problems
- weak skills
- strong skills

and adjust review material accordingly.

## 3.5 Context over isolated words

Vocabulary should normally be learned through:

- sentences
- contexts
- collocations
- idioms
- dialogues
- short texts
- realistic situations

rather than only word → translation cards.

## 3.6 Explain mistakes

For important exercises, do not merely show that an answer is incorrect.

Explain:

- what is wrong
- why it is wrong
- what the correct version is
- what rule applies
- another example

## 3.7 Progressive difficulty

Lessons must progress logically through CEFR levels:

A1 → A2 → B1 → B2 → C1 → C2

while allowing the learner to test into a higher starting position.

## 3.8 Professional design

The product should visually feel like a premium modern SaaS application rather than a student project.
